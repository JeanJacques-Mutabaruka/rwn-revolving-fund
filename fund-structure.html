🏗️ Fund Structure & Key Concepts

Detailed architecture of the revolving fund as documented — loan cycle, governance, financial projections structure — plus the SPV concept and what must be built to close operational gaps.

Loan Cycle — as documented

7-step process RF

From application to re-loaning — the full cycle as described in the Revolving Fund Strategy.

Business plan + loan request. Template / hard copy / email / online / in-person
🔎
Appraisal

Scheduled payments with receipts. Interest from day 1; principal after grace period

⚠️ Grace period calibration needed
The grace period is 3–6 months "depending on income-generating activities." Without a sector map of WSSFs' IGAs, this cannot be calibrated. Agricultural businesses have seasonal cash flows that may require longer or differently structured grace periods than retail or service businesses.

⚠️ Gap: No dedicated Fund Manager role
The current governance structure assigns fund management to existing RWN staff (Program Manager, M&E Officer) on top of their regular responsibilities. As the fund grows to cover multiple districts and dozens of loans simultaneously, this will become a capacity bottleneck. A dedicated Fund Manager role should be planned.
Proposal — SPV Structure

Managing the fund through an SPV

This is a conceptual proposal — not in the source documents — to strengthen the fund's governance, unlock institutional capital, and protect RWN's organisational balance sheet.

What is an SPV and why here?

A Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) is a legally separate entity created by RWN specifically to own and manage the revolving fund. It ring-fences the fund's capital from RWN's operational budget, making it more attractive to institutional investors and protecting RWN from fund liabilities.

  • Ring-fenced capital — fund losses don't touch RWN operations
  • Non-deposit lending institution status — regulated by BNR, lending credibility
  • Can attract concessional loans from development finance institutions (AfDB, IFC, etc.)
  • Separate auditing — cleaner donor reporting and fund performance tracking
  • Enables blended finance structures (grant + concessional debt + equity)
  • Replicable: other NGOs could inject capital into the SPV as co-investors

SPV — Key design questions to answer

Legal form in Rwanda
What legal form? Limited company (Sarl), cooperative, or registered microfinance institution under BNR? Each has different capital requirements, governance rules, and tax treatment.
Capital structure
What share of initial capital comes from: RWN reserves, donor grants, concessional loans, impact investors? What is the target fund size at Year 1, Year 3, Year 5?
Revenue flow
How does interest income flow? What percentage stays in fund for recapitalisation, goes to SPV operations, and goes back to RWN as the fund manager?
Regulatory engagement
BNR (National Bank of Rwanda) engagement plan: when, how, and with which legal advisors. Non-deposit lending institutions in Rwanda require specific licensing.

⚠️ Assumptions needed to fill this table
Before any numbers can be entered, these must be established: (1) Initial fund capital amount, (2) Number of eligible WSSFs and projected take-up rate, (3) Average loan size and average term, (4) Projected default rate based on WSSF profile and comparable programmes, (5) Operational cost per loan (field staff time, monitoring, training), (6) Inflation rate assumption for RWF real value erosion, (7) Growth trajectory (how many new WSS per year?).
Proposal — Business Plan Programme

Helping WSSFs build solid, realistic business plans

A critical gap: WSSFs have no advanced business skills and no money to pay consultants. A business plan is required for eligibility. Without support, this becomes a barrier that excludes the most motivated but least-resourced women.

👩‍🏫
Structured BP Training
Group workshops facilitated by RWN field staff, using the existing business plan template. Covers each section step-by-step, with real examples from Rwandan market context.
Builds on existing RWN capacity building infrastructure
🤖
AI Business Plan Builder
A guided, conversational AI tool (in Kinyarwanda and French) that walks a WSSF through building her business plan — asking questions, generating cost estimates, checking market realism, and producing a formatted document.
→ See Ecosystem page for technical design
✅
BP Review & Quality Gate
Field staff + program team review of submitted business plans before loan committee. Standardised scoring rubric to ensure consistency across districts and reduce subjectivity in approval decisions.
Closes the "re-loaning impact review" gap too
