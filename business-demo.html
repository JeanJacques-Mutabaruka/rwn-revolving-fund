Tax note — Rwanda micro-enterprise regime
WSSFs with annual turnover below RWF 20M typically qualify as micro-enterprises subject to lump-sum tax — lower than 28% CIT. The 28% rate is shown for completeness and for WSSFs who formalise as registered businesses. Actual net profit may be higher.

Conclusions — Maize → Flour

  • Business ONLY viable with milling partner. Without it: loan finances a loss-making activity (−RWF 134,800).
  • Fertiliser (31.9% of cost) is the highest-risk input → priority for BUY-SIDE collective purchasing.
  • RWN 2%/mo saves RWF 25,920 net vs. MFI = 2 bags of fertiliser for next season.
  • Net margin of 58.6% after tax. Business absorbs flour −35% or fertiliser +40% and remains profitable (stress, not default).
  • After 2 cycles, buyer contracts = collateral to graduate to BDF and scale beyond RWN's loan ceiling.

BUY SIDE — Certified seedlings + fungicide
Grafted disease-resistant seedlings from RAB-certified nurseries. Collective fungicide purchase. Quality assurance that individual WSSFs cannot provide alone — a bad seedling batch destroys an entire season.
Partners: RAB nurseries, PROAGRI, Kirehe agro-dealers
🏪
SELL SIDE — Branded paste to institutions
Hotels, restaurants, school canteens. Year-round supply because paste stores 12+ months. "Women-produced" label adds 5–10% price premium with socially conscious buyers.
Buyers: Kigali hotels, NGO kitchens, Gorillas supermarket
🏭
STRATEGIC PARTNER — Processing (non-negotiable)
Guaranteed processing slot within 24h of harvest. Revenue-share model: processor takes 10% of paste output (zero upfront cost for WSSF). Cold chain included. Seasonal harvest calendars shared 4–6 weeks ahead.
Partners: Inyange Industries, MINIMEX, NAEB processors in Burera/Musanze
💰
FINANCE SIDE — Pre-purchase & green finance
Institutional buyers pre-pay 30% for next season's paste = working capital. After 2 cycles, confirmed contracts serve as collateral for Inkomoko or BDF financing of a shared cooperative processing unit.
Partners: Inkomoko, BDF, USAID Feed the Future · Exit: cooperative processing unit

Conclusions — Tomato → Paste

  • Revenue ×4.2 with processing. Without: loss of RWF 111K. With RWN model: net profit of RWF 272.5K after tax.
  • Post-harvest loss is the #1 risk — entirely controlled by processing partner reliability. Guaranteed 24h slots are non-negotiable.
  • Revenue-share model: processor only earns when WSSF earns — fully aligned incentives, zero upfront cost at harvest.
  • Break-even paste price RWF 2,097/kg. Current price (RWF 4,200) is 100% above it — enormous buffer.
  • After 2 cycles, 5–10 WSSF tomato farmers can co-invest in their own pasteurisation unit.

RWN owns the brand, licenses to WSSFs. One collective branding contract covers all soap WSSFs. The branded label unlocks the RWF 500/bar premium — far cheaper via RWN collective contract than individual branding.

Conclusions — Soap Making

  • Tontine at 10%/mo turns a viable business into a loss (−RWF 10,000/month). RWN's 2%/mo generates RWF 40,320/month net profit after tax.
  • Branding is the key value driver: +50% revenue (RWF 1,500 vs 1,000) with only RWF 8K additional packaging cost.
  • Scales efficiently: 200 bars/month nearly doubles net profit — fixed cost leverage is powerful here.
  • Year-round stability makes soap the ideal first-loan IGA and complementary income alongside agricultural IGAs.
  • After 12 months, supply contracts + track record enable graduation to equipment leasing and cooperative production.
